# ADR-0006 — The Laboratory

## How Genesis Closes a Science

Status: **Accepted** (2026-07-29 — founder's dictation merged with the review; the practice
preceded the text: S1-002's close answered all three questions before they had names).

> *« Lorsqu'une nouvelle structure n'est nécessaire que pour relier des structures existantes,
> c'est généralement qu'il manque un rituel, pas un registre. »* — the founder, at this ADR's
> review, withdrawing his own "Meta-001".

## Context

Three precedents told the same story: Genesis-017 refused the Method Observation object;
DN-005 refused the Theory Register; the Pillars died of restating the corpus. What the closes
of S1-001 and S1-002 revealed missing was small: not a layer — a closing ritual, and a map.

## Decision

### Every campaign reduction asks exactly three questions.

1. **What did the world learn?**
2. **What did the laboratory learn?**
3. **What did Genesis learn?**

They are asked at closure — and only there. The rest of the time, they are not posed.

### Every answer has exactly one home.

- **World** → Observations · the science's Research Agenda
- **Laboratory** → the campaign document · `docs/Methods/` (only if it travels)
- **Genesis** → the Journal · an ADR (only if the lesson has become law)

### Every founded science carries a Research Agenda.

One file, beside its charter: the map of its open questions. It invents nothing; it prevents
discoveries from scattering. **Updatable only at a campaign's reduction** — an agenda edited
freely becomes a design space. (Generalised from Science-001's, which proved the property in
its first hour by catching a debt no list had held.)

## What this ADR explicitly refuses

- no Meta Register;
- no permanent Meta Science;
- no duplicated corpus;
- no second history of Genesis.

Meta-campaigns remain possible — the Genesis-017 precedent stands — authorised one at a time,
never standing.
