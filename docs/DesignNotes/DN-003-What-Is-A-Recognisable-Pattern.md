# DN-003 — What is a Recognisable Pattern?

Status: **Discussion.** This note decides no architecture, no representation, no milestone. It
establishes the conditions under which a pattern of the world may honestly receive a name — the
admission rule of Genesis's vocabulary.

> Genesis does not assume that the world's concepts exist. It asks under which conditions they become
> objectively recognisable.

## The two traps

**Naming is not evidence.** The classical engine begins with nouns — `Rock`, `Tree`, `NPC` — and
writes the classes. Genesis inverts the order permanently: *state → behaviour → recognition → name.*
The name always arrives last.

**Recognition is not creation.** To recognise a pattern is to state that a predicate became true of
the world. It creates no object, allocates no identity, stores nothing. `IsTree == true` demonstrates
no `Tree`.

## From seven criteria to two

An earlier sketch proposed seven criteria (state-grounded, deterministic, causally relevant,
vocabulary-late, non-reifying, ablatable, scope-honest). A subtraction pass — the third application
of the project's method, after the ontology arc and the methodology distillation — reduced them:
state-grounded and deterministic are consequences of one criterion; ablatable is the operational test
of the other; causally relevant is its definition; and the remaining three are not properties of
patterns at all, but disciplines of the people who name them.

Two criteria survived. One of them required a repair, recorded here because the repair carries the
note's central insight.

**The attack the reduction had to survive.** As first stated, the second criterion — *"removing the
pattern changes the world's behaviour"* — is nearly vacuous in Genesis. Invariant 6 forbids hidden
state, so *all* state is causally live: altering almost any predicate's grounding state changes the
future. The pattern `CounterA is even` is objective and counterfactually "necessary" (parity changes
integer flux), yet obviously deserves no name. Mere counterfactual difference is free in a fully
explicit, deterministic world.

**The repair.** What separates a Source from `EvenA` is not that its ablation changes *something*,
but that it extinguishes *a statable signature*: presence ⇒ the total grows by `ΣProduction`;
ablation ⇒ the growth vanishes. The name attaches to a **theorem**, not to a difference.

## The criteria

A pattern deserves a name if and only if:

### 1. Objectivity

The pattern is deterministically derivable from the world's declared content — its state, and its
relations where the pattern reads them. Every conforming observer computes the same answer from the
same snapshot. Otherwise it is interpretation, not a property of the world.

### 2. Consequence

A statable behavioural signature appears with the pattern and vanishes with its ablation. Not "the
future differs" — that is free — but "*this* differs, predictably": presence and signature are
demonstrably linked in both directions. Otherwise it is vocabulary, not a concept.

Compactly:

> **A pattern earns a name when the world can demonstrate a theorem about it.**

The word and its theorem arrive together, or not at all.

## What became of the other five

- **State-grounded, deterministic** — consequences of Objectivity.
- **Ablatable** — the operational test of Consequence (the signature's disappearance is what the
  ablation must show; DN-002's ablation test is the special case for behavioural patterns).
- **Causally relevant** — absorbed into Consequence as its definition, repaired from counterfactual
  difference to statable signature.
- **Vocabulary-late, non-reifying, scope-honest** — not properties of the concept but **disciplines
  of the namers**: one forbids naming before demonstration, one forbids a representation, one forbids
  an excessive claim. They bind us, not the world, and they remain binding.

## Worked examples (illustration, not decision)

- **Source** — predicate: `ProductionRate(a) > 0`. Objectivity ✓ (read from declared state).
  Consequence ✓ — theorem: `ΔTotal = ΣProduction`; ablation (`rate = 0`, identical laws) extinguishes
  the growth. *Earns its name.* (Whether and how Genesis-012 demonstrates this remains that
  milestone's question.)
- **`CounterA is even`** — Objectivity ✓; Consequence ✗ — no statable signature attaches to parity.
  *Vocabulary, not a concept. No name.*
- **Entity** — the criteria *derive* its Reserved status rather than asserting it: no theorem of
  identity or continuity has been demonstrated by any world so far. The day such a signature exists,
  Entity will earn activation the same way everything else does.

## What this note turned out to be

The criteria do not speak about patterns in particular. They answer an epistemological question —
*when do we have the right to say a concept exists?* — and the answer is the admission rule for every
future word in the Glossary: objectively derivable from the world, and provably consequential to it.
DN-002 was this rule's special case for behaviour (a phenomenon is a recognisable pattern of
dynamics); DN-003 is the general form. What began as a note about adding a Source ended as a note
about what it costs to deserve a word.

## Non-goals

This note does not design Genesis-012, choose a state representation, activate any Glossary entry, or
define identity, entities, or agents. It defines only the gate through which every future name must
pass.
