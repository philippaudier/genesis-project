## The finding the protocol did not order (the reviewer's personal prediction, filed post-seal,
pre-execution: "the most important result will be in the A(t) logs")

The prediction scores. The logs show a regularity no document had formulated:

> **Two timescales. Activation orbits lock in almost immediately (t ≤ 8 in every world; t = 2 on
> the hardest graph); everything slow happens *inside* a locked orbit.**

The spectral convergence of W2/W4 (tens of ticks), the odd-star bleed of Campaign-001 (hundreds of
ticks at t = (load+1)/2), the divergences — all of them are slow *amplitude* processes riding a
fast-frozen *activation* structure. W1's travelling extinction front is the one observed exception
of structure in motion — and even it lived only 8 ticks. CT-003 assumed episodes exist; the logs
say episodes are **almost the whole story**, and the interesting physics is compressed into the
first handful of ticks.

## Hypotheses

*(kept apart)*

- Orbit lock-in may be governed by sign structure alone (W4's t=1/t=2 deviations are
  scale-*identical*, not just scale-similar): the activation trajectory could be a function of the
  initial sign pattern, computable without amplitudes.
- The travelling front of W1 suggests activation in damped worlds dies by *transport to a
  boundary*, not by in-place decay — untested beyond one chain.
- No mechanism is known that could hold a world in a non-periodic activation régime; whether one
  exists (an aperiodic activation world) is precisely what a real adversary would have hunted for.
