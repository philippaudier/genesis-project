# L-003 — Richer Biographies

*First milestone of Era I, whose charter is: **the world produces a story; the laboratory learns to
read it.** Not to interpret it — to read it. Era discipline now constitutional (Lootbound Lab
Article 6, earned by Run-000002's forensic replay): the reader improves while the world stays
simple.*

## Question

> **Which structures of a life are lost when only first occurrences are kept?**

No longer an intuition — a fact: nine transitions lived, three told (LB-Obs-003).

## What the reader learned (and what it is still forbidden)

The Chronicler, second generation, reports three layers — all facts, none intention:

1. **Firsts** — L-002's irreversible events, kept.
2. **Movement history** — *every* transition of the subject object. The layer whose absence hid
   Run-000002's most telling episode.
3. **Structures** — counts: entries per place, all repairs, entries at the station before the
   first repair, how many times the object moved.

**Vocabulary corrected loudly, citing RD-L6's three specimens** (the third found during this
milestone): *"refused"* (intention) → "left the clearing carrying this sword while the other lay
there"; *"voluntary"* (intention) → "retrieved from the shelter again"; *"superior"* — the
sharpest catch: **the world defines no notion of betterness** (swords differ only by identity,
wear, repairs); "better sword" was designer fiction smuggled into the reader → "the other sword".
Tests now enforce the boundary: the biography must not contain "refused", "Voluntary" or
"superior".

One new distinction surfaced for free: the trace holds ten *Go-Station intents* but only three
*entries* — intents and achievements are different counts. Filed as a fact the reader exposes;
what it means belongs to no one yet.

## The tradition

> **What became observable that wasn't before?**

*The repetitions. For the first time the laboratory can tell not only what happened first, but
what happened again — nine movements where three were told, a second repair nobody knew existed,
and the field's twelve entries: the narrative's crossroads, now countable. The reader stopped
believing that only beginnings are events.*

> **What illusion did the laboratory lose?** *(second tradition question — deposited by the
> founder at this milestone's close, and now standing for every L milestone alongside the first)*

*The illusion that only beginnings count. Time regained its thickness: a life is not a succession
of first times — it is, mostly, a succession of returns. (The founder's restatement of this
milestone's real achievement, adopted: the reader no longer privileges beginnings.)*
