# L-005 — Research Programs

*The last chapter of Era I. Renamed at launch, by the founder, from "Campaigns": a campaign
answers a question; a program pursues a family of questions. With this milestone the laboratory
stops being purely receptive — it learns to decide which questions deserve to be asked, while
keeping its fundamental rule intact: answers come from the world, never from the code.*

## Definition of Done

The laboratory can state, for each active program: the family of questions; the members (debts,
observations, protocols, runs); the current evidence; **the data gap** ("what do we not know AND
cannot learn from the data we have?"); a **proposed next protocol** (proposed — execution always
needs its own authorisation); and a death condition. No program is invented: each must be earned
by the existing corpus.

## The tradition

> **What became observable that wasn't before?**

*The laboratory's own ignorance, organised. For the first time it can state not only what it saw,
but what it lacks — each program ends on a data gap and a proposed way to fill it. The question
"what data do we need now?" has become a documented, auditable act.*

> **What illusion did the laboratory lose?**

*The illusion that questions arrive on their own. Choosing what to ask is an act — and an
undisciplined one would steer the whole enterprise invisibly. Programs make the steering public:
earned members, declared gaps, proposed protocols, death conditions. The laboratory now orients
its research the way it does everything else — on the record.*

## Era I — closed with this milestone

L-001: the world has a memory. L-002: the world has a biography. L-003: the biography has depth.
L-004: the depth has structure. L-005: the structure feeds programs. The era's product — the
language — stands; the maturity criterion stands with it (same trace, same reader, same facts,
different theories welcome). **Era II may open when the founder wills it: the Traveller arrives
before a laboratory that already knows how to watch it walk.**
