# 2026-07-26 — The price of a word

The last attack on DN-003 landed, and closed the reflection.

## The attack

Is a theorem sufficient to deserve a word? The sharpest form of the objection is not an arbitrary
predicate (DN-003's biconditional already blocks patterns with no distinguishing signature) but the
fact that **theorems compose for free**: every legitimate theorem spawns infinitely many derived ones.
"Source-at-a-prime-address" has a real, biconditional, demonstrable signature — carved out of the
Source theorem — and deserves nothing. Admission by theorem alone would not slightly over-admit; it
would flood the vocabulary. (This is Goodman's old riddle — projectible versus gerrymandered
predicates — rediscovered from first principles; compression is its classical resolution.)

## The resolution — and where it lives

**Compression**: a term is justified when it replaces several independent truths with one simpler
concept. "Source" compresses a predicate, an accounting theorem, an ablation result, and a causal
role into one word; "Source-at-a-prime-address" decompresses — longer to state, covering less.

Deliberately **not** a third criterion of DN-003. Objectivity and Consequence are absolute — checkable
per pattern, against one snapshot and one run, timelessly. Compression is *relative to the corpus*: it
compares the language with and without the word, and the corpus changes — a word can become
compressive later, or cease to be. A world-criterion must be timeless; an editorial rule may be
revised. So the rule belongs to the Glossary:

> **A demonstrated theorem is necessary for a new term, but a new term is justified only if it
> compresses the language used to describe the world.**

The world demonstrates. The Glossary decides whether the demonstration deserves a word. Two
responsibilities, two documents.

## The layer reading

The retrospective observation that closes the arc: each of Genesis's great reductions did not merely
remove concepts — it **relocated a responsibility to its legitimate level**:

- laws belong to the kernel;
- theorems belong to the world;
- names belong to the Glossary;
- disciplines belong to the designers.

No layer may do another's work. This is the first-page instinct of the project — *documents have
different levels of authority* — matured into an architecture of knowledge itself. DN-003 is the note
where ontology, epistemology, and language stopped being mixed.

## Close

The reflection is over, by its own criterion: the next subtraction would remove explanatory power,
and the next question ("how is heterogeneous state represented?") is not philosophical but
architectural — RFC-grade, and the last thing standing between the project and the Genesis-012
launch. The thinking stops here. The building resumes.
