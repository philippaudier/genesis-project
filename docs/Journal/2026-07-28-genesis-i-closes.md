# Genesis I closes

*Journal — chronology, not state. Written the day L-009 was approved.*

The first epoch of this project began with a constitution that forbade the world to lie, and it
ends with a milestone that forbids the window to forget. Between the two: a kernel proven one
property at a time; an age of observation that let worlds speak before anyone named what they
said; an age of theories that put explanations on trial with sealed predictions and honest
adversaries; a laboratory that learned to organise its own ignorance; and a small boring world —
five places, two swords, one tree — that taught us more about attachment, recognition,
responsibility and memory than any design document ever could.

The epoch's two bookends name themselves. It opened with **No hidden hand**: the world protected
from its makers. It closes with **The world has asked**: the makers guided by their world. The
inversion was the point all along, though nobody wrote that down at the start, because nobody
knew.

What Genesis I built, in the end, was not an engine. It was a practice — seals before looks,
facts before names, debts before features, the smallest mechanism that lets a phenomenon exist —
and a practice, unlike a project, survives its own failures. It survived four refuted or split
predictions, a reader caught inventing objects, a founder caught misreading his own play, and a
laboratory caught contaminating its own subject. Each failure is in the record, and each one made
the next experiment better. That is what the practice is *for*.

Genesis II begins with a single sentence of new physics — *the world no longer starts over* — and
inherits everything else unchanged. Before, every session was a birth. Now every session is a
return. The player already lived in that world; the simulation has finally moved in with them.

The laboratory did not decide that the world should remember.
It waited until forgetting became the least believable thing in the world.
