# Eras

> **An era is never entered. It is recognised.**

## What this document is

A partition of the Journal, nothing more: where a boundary fell, and what to read. It **cuts**;
it never retells — the Journal tells, and ADR-0006 refuses Genesis a second history. Three lines
per era: name, boundary, references.

Two rules govern it.

> **An era is a reading, not an event. Readings may change while events do not.**

An observation is true or false; a decision stands or is superseded; a boundary between eras may
simply be moved, as boundaries are moved in geology. Nothing here is owed permanence.

> **The present is never named.**

Recognition requires standing on the far side. One cannot see the boundary one is standing on.
This document therefore stops before the present, always — and its last line is the point.

## A correction of record

On 2026-07-28, at the same moment Genesis I was closed, **"Genesis II" was declared open** — in
the present tense. Under the rule this document establishes, that was a naming of the present,
and premature. The declaration is kept as a fact of the record, not ratified as a boundary:
whether an era began that day is for a later reader to recognise. The corpus opened several eras
this way (`Journal/2026-07-26-closing-the-first-field-day.md`, `Milestones/L-006-The-Traveller.md`);
all are kept, none is corrected silently. The laboratory has learned to read its own history
late — which is the only honest way to read it.
