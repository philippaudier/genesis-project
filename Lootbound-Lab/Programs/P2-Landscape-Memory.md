# P2 — Landscape Memory

Status: **Active** (founding program, earned by the corpus)

## The family of questions

Why are certain places remembered? Why does a shelter become "home"? What makes a connective,
eventless place the centre of a told story? What is the relationship between dwelling, crossing,
returning — and remembering?

## Current evidence

The first human narrative named exactly one place — the Field: the most-entered (12) and
least-inhabited (49 of 557 ticks) place of the world. The regularities layer can now state, for
any run, the full factual couple per place: entries × dwell-time. The told place was the crossed
place, not the dwelt place — in one subject, once.

## The data gap

One narrative, one subject, contaminated (post-self-review). The laboratory does not know whether
connective places dominate narratives generally, or whether dwell-time, entry-count, or something
uncounted predicts being told. It also holds zero data on "home": no run has yet shown a shelter
*preference* the reader could count.

## Proposed next protocol

**Rides on H-002** *(P1's proposed protocol — one data collection, two programs served)*: for each
subject, the narrative (asked before any export) is scored against the run's per-place facts —
entries, dwell, transitions. The program's question to the data: *is the told place the crossed
place, the dwelt place, or neither?*

## Experimental cost

Shares H-002 with P1. No additional participants required — only an additional scoring pass over
data already collected.

## Death condition

Paid if a replicated relation links per-place regularities to narrative mention (including a null:
"told places are not predictable from movement facts" — that would be a magnificent finding).
Absorbed into P1 if object-places and landscape-places turn out to obey one mechanism.
