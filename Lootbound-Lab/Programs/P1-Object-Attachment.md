# P1 — Object Attachment

Status: **Active** (founding program, earned by the corpus)

## The family of questions

Why does an object become irreplaceable? When does equipment become a memory? What does keeping,
repairing, returning-to an object mean — and what does taking-and-giving-back mean, now that the
corpus knows that phenomenon exists?

## Members

```
Debts:         RD-L1 (transformed by LB-Obs-003)
Observations:  LB-Obs-001 (bot: not taken) · LB-Obs-003 (human: taken twice, returned twice)
Protocols:     LB-001 The Ordinary Sword (sealed, runnable) · Protocol-H001 (run once)
Runs:          Run-000001 (bot) · Run-000002 (human)
Hypotheses:    anchor vs patina (two rival mechanisms, filed pre-build, both alive)
```

## Current evidence

One bot record: the replacement was not taken (scripted, known out of band). One human record: the
replacement was taken, held 12 then 2 ticks, returned both times; the run ended with the invested
sword in hand (repairs had been spent on it), held 504 of 557 ticks. The two records are not
structurally similar. Four alternative explanations preserved in each observation; none
privileged.

## The data gap

n = 1 human. The anchor/patina discrimination needs keep-decision timestamps correlated with
session events across *several* subjects — and the one human narrative on file is contaminated
(given after self-review). The laboratory cannot learn attachment from a single founder who is
also its director.

## Proposed next protocol

**H-002 — Other Hands** *(proposed, not authorised)*: N ≥ 5 human producers who are not the
founder; Protocol-H001 unchanged except the repaired flaw — the one question is asked and answered
**before** the subject sees any export; anchor/patina scored per subject from timestamps alone.

## Experimental cost

Owns H-002 (N >= 5 human subjects — the register's most expensive protocol). Shares its
collection with P2 at zero marginal cost: the same sessions serve both programs.

## Death condition

Paid if a replicated timestamp signature discriminates anchor from patina (or shows both, with
domains). Dissolved if attachment events stop occurring across many subjects — "no attachment
forms under these conditions" closes the program as a finding, not a failure. And the hierarchy
holds: paying RD-L1 does not kill P1 — the family continues (why do objects become heirlooms?
why are some deliberately abandoned?). The program dies only when its family of questions
disappears.
