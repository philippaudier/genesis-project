# P3 — The Faithful Reader

Status: **Active** (founding program, earned by the corpus — the laboratory's reflexive program)

## The family of questions

How much interpretation is permissible in an observation extractor? How much of a life is lost
when reduced to a biography? What are the families of bias a reader can introduce — and can they
be prevented mechanically rather than caught one by one?

## Members


## Current evidence

Four specimens in three families — exactly the shape measurement-error theory took in physics: not
"the instrument is imprecise" but "here are the *kinds* of error an instrument introduces". Each
specimen was found by a human reading closely; none was found by a mechanism. The reader's
compression loss is quantified once (9 lived / 3 told, v2 closed the gap for movements) — but only
for sword-location; other cells' losses are unmeasured.

## The data gap

Specimen-hunting is manual and luck-dependent. The laboratory has no systematic way to answer:
*does every word in the reader's output map to a world-defined quantity or a declared convention?*
Nor any measure of what the current reader still compresses away (wear trajectories? intent-vs-
entry gaps? the 10-intents-3-entries fact surfaced by accident).

## Proposed next protocol

**The Ontology Audit** *(proposed, not authorised)*: mechanically extract every noun and verb the
reader can emit; map each to (a) a world Kind/Place/value, (b) a declared convention, or (c)
nothing — every (c) is a specimen candidate, reviewed by hand. Second arm: a loss inventory —
for each Kind the world defines, does any reader layer report its full history? Every "no" is a
measured compression.

## Death condition

Paid when the vocabulary boundary is closed mechanically (no word without a mapping) AND the
compression losses are inventoried and *chosen* — RD-L7's demand: the laboratory decides what it
accepts to lose, instead of discovering it. Never fully closed while the reader grows: each new
layer reopens the audit. This program is expected to outlive most others.
